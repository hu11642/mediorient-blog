The Honest Fit Check

  • Right choice: A patient with a PD-1-responsive tumour where the Western PD-1 is not available or too expensive locally.
  • Right choice: A patient who has been through a prior line of PD-1 and is facing a second-line decision.
  • Right choice: A patient with a solid tumour where the PD-1 pathway is not available locally.
  • Not the right choice: A patient with an autoimmune disease where the PD-1 pathway is contraindicated.
  • Not the right choice: A patient whose PD-1 pathway is the same at home and in China.

Frequently Asked Questions

What PD-1 and PD-L1 inhibitors are approved in China?

The NMPA-approved portfolio includes pembrolizumab (Keytruda), nivolumab (Opdivo), cemiplimab (Libtayo), toripalimab, camrelizumab, tislelizumab, sintilimab, and other Chinese-developed PD-1 inhibitors. For PD-L1: atezolizumab (Tecentriq), durvalumab (Imfinzi), and avelumab (Bavencio). The Chinese PD-1 portfolio is the largest in the world, and the cost of the domestic PD-1 inhibitors is 30-60% of the Western equivalent. See the immuno-oncology guide for the full picture.

What are the common side effects of PD-1 inhibitors?

The main side effects are immune-related adverse events (irAEs), which are different from chemotherapy side effects. The most common: fatigue (about 20-30% of patients), rash (10-20%), pruritus (10-20%), diarrhoea (5-10%), hypothyroidism (5-10%), hepatitis (2-5%), pneumonitis (2-5%), and hypophysitis (1-3%). These are manageable with steroids and other immune-modulating treatments. The irAEs are not the same as chemo side effects — they are a different kind of toxicity.

How do PD-1 side effects compare to chemo side effects?

The comparison is not simple. Chemo side effects are predictable, dose-dependent, and reverse: they happen at a known dose, they happen for a known duration, and they resolve after the dose is reduced. PD-1 side effects are immune-related, unpredictable, and can be persistent: they happen at any dose, they can happen months or years after the treatment, and they can require long-term management. This is not a reason to avoid PD-1 — it is a reason to monitor the patient carefully.

What is the cost of a PD-1 inhibitor in China?

For a year of pembrolizumab, the cost is USD $30,000-60,000. For a year of a Chinese-developed PD-1 inhibitor (camrelizumab, tislelizumab, sintilimab), the cost is USD $5,000-15,000. For a patient with limited insurance coverage, the cost difference between the Western PD-1 and the Chinese PD-1 is the reason most international patients choose China. See the cost comparison guide for the year-by-year comparison.
