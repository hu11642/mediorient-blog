The Molecular Profile — The Foundation of the Drug Decision

The molecular profile is the foundation of the drug decision. The specific mutation determines which targeted therapy is effective. For a patient whose molecular profile has not been done, the biopsy is the first step. The biopsy must include the full molecular panel: EGFR, ALK, ROS1, KRAS, NRAS, BRAF, HER2, PD-L1, MSI/MMR, and where relevant BRCA.

The Honest Fit Check

  • Right choice: A patient with an actionable driver mutation where the targeted therapy is not available or too expensive locally.
  • Right choice: A patient whose molecular profile has not been done — the biopsy and the profile are the first step.
  • Right choice: A patient who has been through a prior targeted therapy and is facing a resistance decision.
  • Not the right choice: A patient without an actionable driver mutation — the targeted therapy pathway is not the right drug path.
  • Not the right choice: A patient whose targeted therapy pathway is the same at home and in China.
The bottom line: for a patient with an actionable driver mutation, the Chinese targeted therapy pathway is the same evidence-based protocol at a fraction of the cost. The honest fit check is the piece that decides whether the trip is worth it — and it should be done before the booking.

Frequently Asked Questions

What targeted therapy drugs are available in China?

The NMPA-approved portfolio includes the major EGFR TKIs (osimertinib, afatinib, gefitinib, erlotinib, dacomitinib, furmonertinib, zanicitinib), the ALK TKIs (alectinib, brigatinib, lorlatinib, ceritinib), the anti-VEGF antibodies (bevacizumab, ramucirumab, ziv-aflibercept), the anti-HER2 antibodies (trastuzumab, pertuzumab, T-DM1, trastuzumab deruxtecan), the BCR-ABL TKIs (imatinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, ponatinib, asciminib), and the KRAS G12C inhibitor (sotorasib). The portfolio is not a discount version of the Western portfolio.

How do the costs compare to Western targeted therapy?

For a year of osimertinib, the Chinese cost is USD $15,000-30,000, versus USD $150,000+ in the US. For a year of alectinib, the Chinese cost is USD $15,000-30,000, versus USD $180,000+ in the US. For a year of bevacizumab, the Chinese cost is USD $15,000-30,000, versus USD $50,000+ in the US. For a patient with limited insurance coverage, the cost difference is often the deciding factor. See the cost comparison guide for the year-by-year comparison.

What is the role of the molecular profile in choosing a targeted therapy?

The molecular profile is the foundation of the drug decision. The specific mutation (for example, EGFR exon 19 deletion vs L858R, ALK rearrangement, KRAS G12C, BRAF V600E, HER2 amplification) determines which targeted therapy is effective. For a patient whose molecular profile has not been done, the biopsy is the first step. See the biopsy guide for the process.

What are the side effects of targeted therapy?

The side effects of targeted therapy are different from chemo side effects. The most common: skin rash (for EGFR TKIs), diarrhoea (for EGFR TKIs and anti-VEGF), hypertension (for anti-VEGF), proteinuria (for anti-VEGF), hand-foot syndrome (for some TKIs), and fatigue (for most TKIs). These are manageable with supportive care. The side effects are not the same as chemo side effects — they are a different kind of toxicity.

What should I bring to the first consultation?

The biopsy pathology with the molecular profile, the imaging (DICOM on USB), the prior treatment history (every targeted therapy, dose, cycle, response, reason for stopping), and the English translations of any prior specialist reports. See the translation guide for how to prepare the documents.
