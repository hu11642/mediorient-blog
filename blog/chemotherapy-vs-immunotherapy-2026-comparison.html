When Immunotherapy Is the Right Choice

  • MSI-H/dMMR tumours. Colorectal, gastric, and endometrial cancers with MSI-H are first-line candidates for immunotherapy.
  • PD-L1-high NSCLC. Non-small cell lung cancer with high PD-L1 expression is a first-line candidate for immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy.
  • HER2-negative gastric cancer. Nivolumab + chemotherapy is the first-line for HER2-negative gastric cancer.
  • Triple-negative breast cancer. Pembrolizumab + chemotherapy is the first-line for triple-negative breast cancer with PD-L1 expression.
  • Renal cell carcinoma. PD-1 + CTLA-4 combinations are the first-line for advanced RCC.
  • Melanoma. PD-1 inhibitors are the standard of care for advanced melanoma.

When Chemotherapy Is the Right Choice

  • No actionable immune-related marker. A patient with a tumour that does not have MSI-H, PD-L1+, or TMB-high is often a candidate for chemotherapy first-line.
  • Poor performance status. A patient who is not fit enough for the immunotherapy side effects is often a candidate for a lower-dose chemotherapy.
  • Tumours not typically responsive to immunotherapy. For example, pancreatic cancer is not typically responsive to immunotherapy, and chemotherapy is the first-line.
  • Autoimmune disease. A patient with an active autoimmune disease may not be a good candidate for immunotherapy.

The Combined Approach

For a patient with a PD-L1-high NSCLC or a triple-negative breast cancer, the standard of care is immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy. The combination is more effective than either alone. For a patient with a HER2-positive breast cancer, the combination is trastuzumab + pertuzumab + taxane. For a patient with a HER2-positive gastric cancer, the combination is trastuzumab + fluoropyrimidine + platinum. The combination is the standard of care for many cancer types.

The Honest Fit Check

  • Right choice for immunotherapy: A patient with an actionable immune-related marker (MSI-H, PD-L1+, TMB-high).
  • Right choice for chemotherapy: A patient without an actionable immune-related marker, or a patient with a tumour that is not typically responsive to immunotherapy.
  • Right choice for combination: A patient with a PD-L1-high tumour where the combination is more effective than either alone.
  • Not the right choice for immunotherapy: A patient with an active autoimmune disease where the immunotherapy is contraindicated.
  • Not the right choice for chemotherapy: A patient with an actionable driver mutation where the targeted therapy is the first-line.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is immunotherapy the right choice over chemotherapy?

Immunotherapy is the right choice when the tumour has an actionable immune-related marker — for example, high PD-L1 expression, MSI-H/dMMR status, or a TMB-high profile. For a patient with MSI-H colorectal cancer, immunotherapy is the first-line standard. For a patient with high PD-L1 lung cancer, immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy is the standard of care. For a patient without an actionable immune-related marker, chemotherapy is often the first-line standard.

When is chemotherapy the right choice over immunotherapy?

Chemotherapy is the right choice when the tumour does not have an actionable immune-related marker, or when the patient's performance status is too poor to tolerate the immunotherapy side effects, or when the tumour is not a typical immunotherapy-responsive type. For a patient with a triple-negative breast cancer or a squamous cell lung cancer without high PD-L1, chemotherapy is often the first-line standard.

What are the side effects of immunotherapy vs chemotherapy?

The side effects are different. Chemo side effects are predictable, dose-dependent, and reversible: nausea, fatigue, myelosuppression, alopecia. Immunotherapy side effects are immune-related, unpredictable, and persistent: fatigue, rash, hypothyroidism, hepatitis, pneumonitis, hypophysitis. Neither is 'better' — they are different kinds of toxicity. For a patient who is a good candidate for both, the decision depends on the tumour's actionable marker, not on the side-effect profile.

What is the cost of immunotherapy in China?

For a year of a Chinese-developed PD-1 inhibitor (camrelizumab, tislelizumab, sintilimab), the cost is USD $5,000-15,000. For a year of pembrolizumab (Keytruda), the cost is USD $30,000-60,000. For a year of chemotherapy, the cost is USD $5,000-15,000 depending on the regimen. For a patient with limited insurance coverage, the cost difference between the Western PD-1 and the Chinese PD-1 is often the deciding factor.
