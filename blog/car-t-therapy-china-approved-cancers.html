Frequently Asked Questions

What CAR-T products are NMPA-approved in China?

Two: axicabtagene ciloleucel (Yescarta, for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and mantle cell lymphoma) and tisagenlecleucel (Kymriah, for B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma). In addition, there is a domestic CAR-T product (rex-ming, developed by Legend Biotech and sold in China under a different name) that is also NMPA-approved for relapsed/refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. The Chinese CAR-T portfolio is real and approved.

How much does CAR-T cost in China?

For the approved CAR-T products, the total cost (the product itself plus the hospitalisation and the supportive care) is in the ¥1,200,000-¥2,500,000 range, or USD $165,000-350,000. This is typically 30-60% of the US equivalent, which is USD $350,000-500,000 for the same product. The cost is not the piece that changes the outcome — the pathway decision is.

Who is eligible for CAR-T therapy?

For the approved indications: patients with relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), mantle cell lymphoma, or B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (B-ALL) who have failed at least two prior lines of systemic therapy. For a patient with a different cancer type — for example, solid tumours or multiple myeloma — the pathway is a clinical trial, not the approved indication. See the clinical trials guide for what is actually open.

What are the side effects of CAR-T?

The two main side effects: cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and immune effector cell-related neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS). CRS is a febrile, hypotensive, and hypoxic syndrome that occurs in the first 2-3 weeks after the infusion. ICANS is a neurological syndrome that occurs in the first 2-4 weeks. Both are manageable with supportive care, but they require hospitalisation and monitoring. The CAR-T programme at a Chinese cancer centre has the infrastructure for this.

What should I bring to the first consultation?

The biopsy pathology (with the B-cell receptor expression status), the imaging (DICOM on USB), the prior treatment history (every line of therapy, dose, cycle, response, reason for stopping), the B-cell markers (CD19 status), and the English translations of any prior specialist reports.
